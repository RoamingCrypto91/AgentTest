# What to sell, and in what order

Blunt notes, not a business plan. The instrument exists because of the
sequencing problem below, and the sequencing problem is the actual constraint.

## The problem with selling "community systems"

Nobody wakes up wanting a system. They wake up knowing their server is quiet
and not knowing why. "I build community systems" asks the buyer to do the
translation, and buyers do not.

Worse: value-based pricing needs a baseline. Charging on impact means agreeing
what the number was before and what it is after, and until an instrument
exists there is no before. That is why this is the first build and not the
tenth.

## The sequence

**1. The audit is the wedge, not the product.** Fixed scope, fixed price,
delivered as one report. Once the instrument exists it costs an hour of real
work. Price it low enough that it is an obvious yes and high enough that it is
not free — a few hundred euro is the shape of it. Its job is not the revenue.
Its job is to establish the baseline, prove competence with numbers instead of
adjectives, and earn the right to the next conversation.

**2. The rebuild is the product.** A fixed-scope engagement against the
binding constraint the audit found, priced against the baseline it
established, with a re-audit at the end that either shows the movement or does
not. That is what impact pricing actually requires, and it is now possible.

**3. The monitoring is the retainer.** The audit re-run monthly, priced low.
It is nearly free to deliver and it is the thing that catches a slide in the
month it happens rather than the quarter after.

**4. The benchmark set is the asset.** Every audit run adds a community to the
distribution. Ten of them and the report stops saying "this is low by my
judgement" and starts saying "this is in the bottom quartile of the
communities we have measured". Nobody who has not done the audits can say
that, and it gets stronger every time it is used. `vitals benchmark` is that
mechanism. Keep the JSON from every audit.

## Distribution, which is the real constraint

Two clients found through job ads is not a business, it is two jobs. The
instrument changes what cold outreach can look like:

> I ran the numbers on your server. Three percent of your eight thousand
> members have ever posted, and of everyone who joined in January, four
> percent were still talking in March. Here is the report.

That is not a pitch, it is a finding, and it is the only kind of first message
that gets answered. It works because the analysis is already done before the
conversation starts.

The other half is publishing the anonymised findings. "I measured eleven
creator Discords and this is what kills them" is a piece of content that can
only be written by somebody who did it, which is the whole point.

## The thing to be honest about

An instrument is not a moat. Anyone can write these nineteen measures once
they know which nineteen to write. What is defensible is the accumulated
distribution, the intervention library that has been tested against real
communities and revised, and the fact of having done it. All three come from
running audits, so run audits.

## And the uncomfortable part

Two client roles filling early mornings, the middle of the day and late
evenings is not a foundation to build on, it is the reason there is no time to
build. One of them should become the lab: the place where the instrument gets
pointed at real data and the interventions get tested. The other should be
deliberately shrunk or ended on a date chosen in advance rather than on the
day it becomes unbearable.
